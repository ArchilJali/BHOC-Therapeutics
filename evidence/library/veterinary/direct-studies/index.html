Historical canine archive · Interpretation layer

From 885 reports to three research questions.

Oxyglobin Solution - Case Listing Book II is a manufacturer-collected descriptive archive, not a controlled efficacy trial. It contains 885 veterinary reports from 1998-1999 and 146 selected printed canine vignettes. Its recurring clinical pattern is oxygen-carrying support used as a bridge while definitive treatment addressed the cause.

Question 1

When can oxygen support create time?

Define the window in which immediate oxygen-carrying support may bridge to hemorrhage control, surgery, disease-specific therapy, compatible blood or endogenous erythropoiesis.

Question 2

Which endpoints capture function?

PCV measures red-cell volume, not plasma hemoglobin. Total hemoglobin, circulation, unloading, tissue demand and clinical physiology must be considered together.

Question 3

What can translate beyond dogs?

Canine history can define research questions. It cannot establish formulation, dose, rate, safety, efficacy or approval in another species.

Evidence boundary: descriptive, product-specific and selection-prone. The archive is separate from the controlled FDA field trial and is not evidence of BHOC efficacy. The full scan is not republished while redistribution rights remain unconfirmed.

Hemoglobin & other oxygen-carrying pigments

Nature uses more than one hemoglobin strategy.

Hemoglobin

Iron-containing heme protein and the central oxygen carrier across almost all vertebrate life.

Hemocyanin

Copper-based oxygen carrier found in many molluscs and arthropods.

Hemerythrin

Non-heme iron oxygen-binding protein found in several marine invertebrate groups.

Chlorocruorin

Greenish extracellular respiratory pigment found in certain marine polychaete worms.

Erythrocruorin

Large extracellular hemoglobin complex found in annelids and some other invertebrates.

Antarctic icefish

A notable vertebrate exception: adult crocodile icefish lack functional hemoglobin and rely on specialized cold-water physiology.

Nature Is a Great EngineerHeme and chlorophyll are built on closely related tetrapyrrole chemistry. Their molecular architecture follows the same fundamental biological design, but with a remarkable change at the center: Heme coordinates iron (Fe). Chlorophyll coordinates magnesium (Mg). Different metals. Related molecular architecture. Different functions.
The same extraordinary engineering logic of nature.
