William W. Muir: oxygen delivery, perfusion and veterinary oxygen therapeutics

The work of Professor William W. Muir III, DVM, MSc, PhD, traces a long investigation of the difference between circulating oxygen, effective perfusion, gas exchange and tissue oxygen availability. The publications below are authored or coauthored by Muir; each is counted once by its DOI or PMID. This page is a curated reading route, not an additional publication record.

Development of the research

1975 · Respiratory physiology · PMID 1111380Ventilatory alterations in normal horses in response to changes in inspired oxygen and carbon dioxide

Muir, Moore and Hamlin measured how ventilation changes with inspired oxygen and carbon dioxide, an early foundation for later questions about oxygen delivery.

Original record ↗
1986 · Cardiac output and gas exchange · PMID 3740627Dobutamine-induced augmentation of cardiac output does not enhance respiratory gas exchange in anesthetized recumbent healthy horses

Swanson and Muir found that increasing cardiac output did not necessarily improve respiratory gas exchange in their experimental conditions. This is a specific physiological finding, not a general claim about all patients.

Original record ↗
1999 · Educational publication · Muir CVTissue in Desperate Need of Oxygen

A historical marker of Muir's interest in tissue-level oxygen availability. Cited in his curriculum vitae as Veterinary Forum 1999;16(4):50–57. An independently verified online original was not established.

2000 · HBOC-301 / Oxyglobin · PMID 10999460The effects of a hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier (HBOC-301) on left ventricular systolic function in anesthetized dogs

Muir, de Morais and Constable investigated ventricular performance during HBOC-301 infusion. Vascular resistance and cardiac output responses reinforced the need to measure more than oxygen content.

2003 · HBOC physiology review · PMID 12683610Hemoglobin Solutions and Tissue Oxygenation

Muir and Wellman integrated oxygen affinity, facilitated diffusion, viscosity, nitric oxide, capillary perfusion and tissue oxygenation. This review is already indexed as one unique publication in the scientific library.

Catalogue record → · DOI ↗
2009 · Resuscitation rheology · PMID 19430240Evaluation of hyperviscous fluid resuscitation in a canine model of hemorrhagic shock: a randomized, controlled study

Cooper, Bateman and Muir investigated viscosity and resuscitation in hemorrhagic shock, connecting the physical characteristics of infused fluids with cardiovascular responses.

DOI ↗
2011 · Vital-organ tissue oxygenation · PMID 21330945Vital organ tissue oxygenation after serial normovolemic exchange transfusion with HBOC-201 in anesthetized swine

Muir and colleagues directly evaluated oxygen tension in organs during progressive hemodilution. The study is already present in the BHOC evidence catalogue.

DOI ↗
2014 · OC99 and oxygen debt · DOI 10.1155/2014/864237Dose-Dependent Hemodynamic, Biochemical, and Tissue Oxygen Effects of OC99 following Severe Oxygen Debt Produced by Hemorrhagic Shock in Dogs

Muir, Carl W. Rausch and colleagues measured tissue oxygen tension, lactate, base excess and hemodynamic effects during dose-ranging evaluation. Benefits and pulmonary pressure responses were considered together.

DOI ↗
2015 · Equine anaesthesia review · PMID 24612155Oxygenation, oxygen delivery and anaesthesia in the horse

Hubbell and Muir returned to the practical distinction between oxygenation, delivery and the consequences of anaesthesia in horses.

Scientific context and catalogue accounting

The publications are cross-disciplinary: HBOC-301 and HBOC-201 relate to product evidence; diffusion and perfusion relate to Oxygen Diffusion & Microcirculation; veterinary physiology and shock models are relevant to the Veterinary view. A scientific paper must not be counted twice merely because it is discoverable through several sections.
