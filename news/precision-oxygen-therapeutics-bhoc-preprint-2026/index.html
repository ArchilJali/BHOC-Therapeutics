New Preprint Defines the BHOC and Precision Oxygen Therapeutics Framework

Carl W. Rausch and Archil Jaliashvili have published a new preprint proposing a function-first framework for Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carriers and Precision Oxygen Therapeutics.

23 September 2026Preprint posted 22 September 2026Version 1 · not peer-reviewed
Precision Oxygen Therapeutics: From Blood Substitutes to the Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier (BHOC) Framework for Controlled Microvascular and Tissue-Level Oxygen Delivery

Carl W. Rausch · Archil Jaliashvili · Preprints.org · 2026 · DOI: 10.20944/preprints202609.1886.v1 ↗

From replacement terminology to physiological function

The paper argues that “blood substitute” is too broad a description for technologies designed to support a specific physiological function. Blood is a complex biological system. Oxygen transport and delivery are functions within that system that can be measured, characterized and investigated independently.

Within this framework, the authors define BHOC — Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier — as a biologically derived or biologically engineered hemoglobin-mediated oxygen-delivery material or therapeutic whose relevant molecular, oxygen-binding, pharmacological, vascular, redox and manufacturing properties can be quantitatively characterized and reproducibly controlled.

Precision Oxygen Therapeutics

The proposed framework moves the scientific question toward where, when, how much and how effectively oxygen is delivered. It emphasizes microvascular distribution, oxygen affinity and unloading, blood flow, diffusion, metabolic demand, vascular interactions and tissue-level oxygen availability.

  • Blood is a system. Oxygen delivery is a function.
  • BHOC is not proposed as a replacement for the established HBOC classification or the broader term oxygen carrier.
  • The paper presents terminology and a testable research agenda for controlled microvascular and tissue-level oxygen delivery.
