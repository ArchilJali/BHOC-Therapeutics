Predicting prehospital blood transfusion need remains difficult

A study of 25,473 trauma patients found that simple prehospital criteria identified a group in which 52% received blood within four hours. The best-performing criterion reached a PPV of 56%.

▥Why it mattersEmergency systems still have to decide who may need rapid oxygen-carrying support before complete hospital diagnostics are available. Patient selection, timing and tissue-level oxygenation remain central questions for future oxygen-delivery strategies.

8 September 2026Blood Supply · Public Health

Australia and Wales show how quickly donor-blood supply can tighten

Official blood-service reports show how seasonal illness, weather, donor availability and inventory dynamics can place pressure on mature national blood systems.

☰Why it mattersDonor blood remains essential, but availability depends on collection, testing, blood-group compatibility, storage, transport and timing. That is why complementary shelf-ready oxygen-delivery technologies are strategically relevant.
Full analysis

Australia: Australian Red Cross Lifeblood issued an urgent national appeal on 4 August for 12,000 additional donors after ongoing shortages of type O and A blood. Lifeblood reported that O-positive and O-negative supplies remained low and that A-positive stocks had also fallen. Its national blood-supply page, updated 4 September, continued to classify O-negative as Low, with O-positive and A-positive requiring additional donations.

Wales: On 17 July, the Welsh Blood Service appealed for donors to help fill 13,000 summer appointments after heatwaves caused around 1,400 donation appointments to be cancelled. The service said it aims to maintain at least seven days of stock for every blood group and was particularly encouraging A-positive, O-positive, O-negative and B-negative donors to come forward.

These events are not failures of transfusion medicine. Donor blood remains essential and life-saving. They illustrate a structural constraint: blood must be donated, typed, tested, processed, stored, transported and available in the right group at the right place and time.

BHOC perspectiveThe strategic question is not “blood versus blood substitute.” BHOC is positioned as a Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier within Precision Oxygen Therapeutics, focused on the oxygen-delivery function, complementing transfusion systems when compatible donor blood is unavailable, delayed or logistically constrained.
