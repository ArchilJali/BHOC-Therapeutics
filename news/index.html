When blood was not an option: HBOC-201 and U.S. Expanded Access

The published clinical record documents multiple survivors after HBOC-201 was used in life-threatening anemia when conventional red-cell transfusion was unavailable or could not be used, including Jehovah’s Witness patients.

O₂Key evidenceA multicenter compassionate-use series included 54 patients with median hemoglobin 4 g/dL; 23 survived to hospital discharge. Additional case reports document survival after traumatic hemorrhage, leukemia and other critical anemia scenarios.

14 September 2026Rare Blood · Paediatric Critical Care · Blood Logistics

One child required every compatible blood unit available nationwide

Nine-year-old Qasim Rizwan had a rare red-cell profile that included the r’r’ phenotype. NHS Blood and Transplant sent all six matching frozen units held in its national bank, followed by another unit from Colindale, and called a rare donor specifically for him.

?The questionHow many patients with rare or complex blood profiles face the same risk without reaching the news? And what happens to the next patient when every compatible stored unit has already been used?
BHOC perspectiveCompatible donor blood remains essential and saved this child. But when oxygen delivery is failing, health should not depend only on whether the exact blood is already stored and close enough to deliver. It is time to develop and test a bridge solution that can support oxygen delivery while compatible blood is located, prepared and transported.

8 September 2026Blood Supply · Public Health

Australia and Wales show how quickly donor-blood supply can tighten

Official blood-service reports show how seasonal illness, weather, donor availability and inventory dynamics can place pressure on mature national blood systems.

☰Why it mattersDonor blood remains essential, but availability depends on collection, testing, compatibility, storage, transport and timing.
Full analysis

Australia: Lifeblood issued an urgent national appeal on 4 August for 12,000 additional donors after shortages of type O and A blood.

Wales: The Welsh Blood Service appealed for donors to fill 13,000 summer appointments after heatwaves caused around 1,400 donation appointments to be cancelled.

These are not failures of transfusion medicine. They illustrate a structural constraint: donor blood must be donated, typed, tested, processed, stored, transported and available in the right group at the right place and time.

BHOC perspectiveThe strategic question is not “blood versus blood substitute.” BHOC is positioned within Precision Oxygen Therapeutics around the oxygen-delivery function when appropriate donor blood is unavailable, delayed or logistically constrained.
