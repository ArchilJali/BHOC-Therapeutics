HOPE and NMP Are Becoming Clinical Platforms in Liver Transplantation

Two real-world studies show how oxygenated machine perfusion is being used for donor liver assessment, higher-risk graft selection and measurable viability endpoints.

13 September 2026 · Research update

An international cohort compared 954 livers treated with normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) with 1,202 livers treated with hypothermic oxygenated perfusion (HOPE). In the reported NMP cohort, death-censored graft survival remained above 96% for DBD grafts and above 94% for DCD grafts through three years. After risk adjustment, outcomes were similar between NMP and HOPE.

A second multicentre registry study reviewed 545 NMP liver transplants across nine centres. NMP was used for DCD grafts, steatotic livers, older donor organs and cases involving expected prolonged cold ischaemia. Reported 90-day death-censored graft failure was 1.7%.

Oxygenated machine perfusion is becoming a clinical platform.It supports organ assessment, viability testing and the use of higher-risk donor grafts.

These studies do not evaluate BHOC and do not demonstrate BHOC efficacy. They identify a research environment in which oxygen availability, tissue-level injury, perfusate design and viability can be measured directly.
