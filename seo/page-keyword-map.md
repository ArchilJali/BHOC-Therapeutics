## BHOC acronym and entity disambiguation strategy

### Problem confirmed 4 September 2026

The bare acronym `BHOC` is ambiguous in public search. An unrelated entity, **Behavioral Healthcare Outcomes Consortium**, owns a strong exact-match LinkedIn URL `/company/bhoc/` and can rank prominently for the naked acronym. This is not our Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier entity.

Third-party posts about our BHOC, including Mary Kiige's LinkedIn content, can also outrank Archil Jaliashvili's own posts because older indexed URLs may have stronger crawl history, engagement and external references. This is an authority/distribution issue, not evidence that Google fails to index Archil.

### Strategic implication

Do not use the naked query `BHOC` as the only short-term SEO KPI. Track it, but first strengthen the semantic entity that Google must associate with our work:

**BHOC = Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier**

The near-term goal is to dominate oxygen/hemoglobin-qualified BHOC searches before treating the bare acronym as the main battleground.

## Blood substitute / artificial blood search-bridge strategy

`blood substitute` and `artificial blood` are established public and scientific search terms with decades of literature, but they are imprecise for technologies whose primary function is oxygen transport rather than replacement of whole blood.

Desired semantic bridge:

**blood substitute / artificial blood -> hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier (HBOC) -> oxygen therapeutics -> BHOC / Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier**

Ownership rules:

1. Do **not** change the BHOC homepage primary intent to `blood substitute` or `artificial blood`.
2. `/technology/` should own the concise corrective explanation: the field has historically been described using these terms, but BHOC is framed around oxygen-delivery function rather than replacing blood as a whole.
3. `BHOC-platform` should own the deeper historical/evidence intent for generic searches such as `blood substitute HBOC`, `artificial blood HBOC`, `Hemopure blood substitute` and the evolution from legacy terminology to oxygen therapeutics.
4. When enough unique evidence is assembled, create one substantive BHOC-platform terminology/history page rather than multiple thin keyword pages. Recommended intent: **Blood Substitutes, Artificial Blood, HBOCs and Oxygen Therapeutics - Terminology and Evidence**.
5. Link that evidence page from the corporate Technology page using a descriptive anchor such as `blood substitute and artificial blood terminology`.
6. Do not keyword-stuff `blood substitute` or `artificial blood` across unrelated application pages, veterinary pages or homepage sections.
