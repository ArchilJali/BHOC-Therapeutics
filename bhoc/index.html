BHOC

BHOC - Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier

What is new and why we exist. BHOC Therapeutics uses BHOC to mean Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier: a development framework centered on the oxygen-delivery function of hemoglobin rather than on the idea of replacing blood as a complete biological system.

What is BHOC?

Blood is a system. Oxygen delivery is a function.

Hemoglobin is the principal oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells. A hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier moves that oxygen-carrying function outside the intact red-cell framework through a specifically engineered hemoglobin formulation.

BHOC builds on the scientific history of hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers (HBOCs) while emphasizing biological function, tissue oxygen delivery, evidence and practical deployment. BHOC is not presented as a substitute for every function of blood.

What is new

From the blood-replacement question to the oxygen-delivery question.

For decades, the field was often framed around whether an engineered product could replace blood or red blood cells. That framing is too broad. Blood provides oxygen transport, hemostasis, immune functions, buffering, volume support and many other biological functions.

The BHOC framework asks a narrower and more actionable question: when oxygen delivery becomes critically limited, can the oxygen-transport function be supported independently, predictably and in the clinical or operational setting where it is needed?

This is the reason for the shift toward Precision Oxygen Therapeutics: evaluate oxygen delivery by function, physiology, context and evidence rather than by the assumption that one technology should replace the whole blood system.

Terminology

HBOC, Artificial Blood & Blood Substitute Terminology

HBOC means hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier, the established scientific term used across decades of literature. Artificial blood and blood substitute are older and still widely searched terms, but they can imply replacement of the many functions of blood. BHOC Therapeutics retains these expressions for historical, scientific and search discovery while using Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier and Precision Oxygen Therapeutics for current positioning.

Evolution & oxygen delivery

Evolution built a system around hemoglobin.

Hemoglobin is more than a molecule that happens to carry oxygen. Across vertebrate evolution, globin-based oxygen binding became a highly successful molecular solution, and multiple layers of biology developed around it to protect, regulate and deploy that function.

The red blood cell is one of those layers. It packages hemoglobin inside a specialized cellular environment. Blood flow, vascular tone, ventilation, erythropoiesis, microcirculation, tissue diffusion and metabolic demand add further levels of control. The result is not a single oxygen carrier but a multi-level oxygen-delivery architecture.

Different species adapt different parts of that architecture to their physiology and environment. The details vary, but the biological problem remains the same: oxygen must be loaded, transported, released and ultimately made available to living tissue. Understanding those layers helps explain both the power of the natural red-cell system and why BHOC focuses on one defined function within it: oxygen delivery.

01 · Molecular level

Hemoglobin: the oxygen-binding core

Hemoglobin is the molecular engine at the center of red-cell oxygen transport. Adult human hemoglobin A is a tetramer made of two alpha and two beta globin chains. Each subunit contains one heme group with ferrous iron (Fe2+), so one hemoglobin tetramer can reversibly bind up to four oxygen molecules.

The important feature is not only capacity but controlled affinity. Oxygen binding is cooperative: binding at one heme changes the conformational equilibrium of the tetramer and increases the probability of oxygen binding at the remaining sites. This produces the sigmoidal oxygen-dissociation curve that supports loading at higher oxygen partial pressure and unloading as tissue oxygen pressure falls.

P50, the oxygen partial pressure at 50% hemoglobin saturation, is a practical expression of oxygen affinity. Lower P50 means higher oxygen affinity; higher P50 means lower affinity and, under the appropriate physiological conditions, easier unloading. Hemoglobin affinity is therefore not a fixed number. pH and carbon dioxide through the Bohr effect, temperature and red-cell 2,3-BPG all influence the loading-unloading balance.

The chemical state of the heme iron also matters. Ferrous Fe2+ supports reversible oxygen binding; oxidation to ferric Fe3+ produces methemoglobin, which cannot reversibly bind oxygen. The natural red cell therefore does more than contain hemoglobin: it maintains the molecular environment in which hemoglobin remains functional.

Why this matters for BHOC: moving hemoglobin outside the intact red-cell system is not simply a question of putting hemoglobin into solution. A Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier must be evaluated as an engineered oxygen-delivery system in which oxygen affinity, reversible binding, oxidation state, molecular stability and the formulation environment are product-specific design variables. That is the bridge from natural hemoglobin biology to HBOC/BHOC technology.

Historical Evolution

Evolution of Blood Transfusion and Oxygen Therapeutics.

For centuries, medicine removed blood to treat disease. Then it learned to replace blood to save life, identify compatibility, build donor systems and search for artificial blood and blood substitutes. The history of HBOCs, Hemopure, HBOC-201, Oxyglobin, Biopure, Perftoran and newer oxygen-carrier applications shows how the scientific question gradually moved from replacing blood as a whole toward understanding and supporting specific biological functions.

The full article follows the main steps from early transfusion and Landsteiner's blood-group discovery to blood banking, HIV-era safety, artificial oxygen carriers, HBOC development, donor-system constraints, modern EMS and the BHOC function-first framework.
